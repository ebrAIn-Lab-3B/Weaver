## Tensions and decisions to resolve

1. **One decider per state and several actions.** REQ-012 currently requires exactly one decider for each state. PALab REQ-011 identifies a person for an individual decision or review request, and a card may have multiple concurrent actions. These statements do not establish the same scope. Decide whether REQ-012's decider governs a transition or a particular action, and how multiple requests are represented; do not assume one party must perform every role. Change a requirement only after that decision.
2. **Owner, proposal and observed work.** A task-file owner, Issue assignee, Project owner, proposed assignee, and actual actor are different facts. Their sources may disagree or be unavailable. Preserve each source and its freshness; do not turn a proposal or label into delivery, start, approval, or completion. Agree what produces structured, linked evidence before treating free text as authoritative.
3. **Human approval and organization policy.** REQ-002 and DEC-017 currently describe a human approval gap for verified agent work. PALab REQ-011 distinguishes an approval for a specific risk from an ordinary decision or resume request. These are different scopes and do not by themselves establish a contradiction, but applying a universal human-approval rule in a policy-neutral collaboration model needs explicit review. Record whether existing behavior stays, becomes configurable, or needs a requirement/decision change; do not silently change it.
4. **Two safety boundaries.** Weaver's per-write confirmation and on-demand restrictions in accepted DEC-008 and DEC-012 govern Weaver's first steward. PALab's router has its own operating policy and manual and Ready paths. Neither a Weaver confirmation nor a router claim establishes the other's execution or concurrency guarantees. Any unattended Weaver behavior needs its own decision; router guarantees require their own design and tests, including recovery and stale approvals.
5. **Agent reach and evidence.** DEC-017 acts on label reach and keeps other reach types descriptive; TASK-016 records a possible webhook-style direction, not an accepted protocol. Agree a versioned, policy-neutral contract and actor attribution with PALab, including the behavior for shared accounts and unavailable evidence. Do not equate the reach mechanism with evidence of receipt.

## Whether to consolidate requirements

The collaboration outcome can be reviewed as one initiative, but the current draft REQ-023 is specifically a read-only evidence view. It does not contain the acceptance criteria for a remote handoff, a confirmed assignment write, or the prerequisites for unattended assignment. Simply moving the three tasks to it would lose those obligations and misrepresent its scope.

| Current requirement and criteria | Responsibility to preserve if reorganized | Candidate treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REQ-002 AC-005 and AC-006; TASK-016 | Durable remote-agent handoff with returned branch, PR and test evidence; try the same contract with at least two agents. | Keep until a collaboration requirement explicitly includes equivalent handoff criteria and the human-owned decision is revisited. REQ-002's other criteria and delivered work remain traceable. |
| REQ-012 AC-001 through AC-004; TASK-071 | Decider, holder, proposal, confirmed application and surfacing human-needed cards. The scope of one decider per state is still an open question. | Keep until the proposed outcome and confirmed write have explicit criteria and the decider question is settled. Do not substitute a read-only evidence view for application. |
| REQ-012 AC-005; TASK-072 | Automatic application remains out of scope until the listed prerequisites are settled. | Retain as an explicit guard in any new requirement; coordinate separately with router design and tests. Moving the task does not authorize automation. |
| Draft REQ-023 | Read-only evidence, human action visibility, sources, conflicts and verification. | Use as the candidate collaboration evidence requirement, linked to the existing requirements; broadening it into an end-to-end requirement is a separate product choice. |

Recommended for this iteration: use this assessment as the shared work map, leave each task linked to its existing requirement, and coordinate the evidence requirement through draft PR #361. This avoids silently changing REQ-023's read-only boundary or dropping earlier acceptance criteria while the PALab contract and decider scope are unsettled. It is a working organization, not a decision against consolidation.

If a single broader requirement is chosen after policy alignment, first draft its outcome and explicit criteria for handoff, confirmed assignment, evidence visibility, and the automation guard. Map every existing criterion and task to the new criteria and preserve the old requirement's remaining scope, story and completion evidence. Criteria IDs in Weaver are assigned by list order, and a task has one `requirement` reference with criteria local to it; merely listing a task under two requirements does not create a supported second relationship. For an actual move, update the task's `requirement` and `criteria`, both requirements' task lists and issue references, and any affected stories and decisions together; verify joins and acceptance coverage before merging. Preserve the three existing issues, their owners and history, and do not lift TASK-016's deferral or DEC-008/DEC-012's boundaries by the move. Keep PALab router implementation under its owning repository with explicit cross-links, and agent-base adoption as a later goal.

## Proposed sequence

1. Compare these questions and representative human and agent scenarios with PALab; agree the producer records and source semantics. Retain open items and their owners as decisions, not facts inferred from the UI.
2. Revisit the deferral and scope of TASK-016 with its human owner. The REQ-023 read-only view and TASK-129 may progress independently where their sources are already explicit.
3. Compare the router's existing controls and operational evidence with the paths being used. Track a new router change only for a demonstrated gap or a specifically required stronger guarantee. TASK-072 concerns Weaver's automatic-assignment prerequisites and does not establish router behavior. Preserve confirmation and deployment boundaries until explicitly changed.
4. Verify with synthetic scenarios: human-to-agent and agent-to-human handoff, concurrent human actions, a changed PR head, duplicate and simultaneous signals, stale approval, missing evidence and recovery. Compare records with the card at each observed stage; list untested stages.
5. Adoption in agent-base is a subsequent goal after policy alignment and validation, not an implicit change to that repository now.

## Proposed simplification following review

- **Resume signal.** PALab [PR #147](https://github.com/blcktgr73/palab-platform/pull/147) retained `approved` as a shared resume signal for an ordinary Decision hold and a risk-gated hold, matching the current router. The signal alone must not be presented as proof of the human decision, the actor's identity, or approval of a different risk scope. For a gated action, the operator checks the actual risk approval and scope in the existing Issue before resuming. The router checks the label and does not independently bind it to an approval record, revision or scope.
- **What the HealthBridge trial proved.** Existing Issue #9 shows the development-agent report and a review-agent verification comment, and PR #10 was merged. The user also confirmed the collaboration field display. Treat those observed stages as confirmed, rather than demanding an entire repeat. Only investigate a specific missing fact when needed: the exact agent read history, independent delivery/start evidence, post-merge verification, and any behavior whose recorded source is absent remain unknown. The router unit tests are evidence for their exercised cases, not the whole deployment.
- **TASK-016.** Keep the human-owned decision and its existing deferral until its owner chooses to resume it. Start with PALab's Issue-based handoff and compare it with the two-agent portability criterion; specify only the minimum interoperable context and returned evidence that the existing contract cannot express. Do not introduce a mandatory duplicate handoff document or choose a webhook protocol in advance.
- **TASK-071.** Keep it under REQ-012 for now. First decide whether a decider belongs to a transition or a particular action when several actions coexist. Reuse observed owner and links; propose an assignee only where a real need is established. The current REQ-012 confirmed-write criterion remains in force until explicitly revised; a read-only card does not satisfy it.
- **TASK-072.** Keep automatic Weaver assignment deferred. Reuse existing router controls and test evidence rather than redesigning them. Revisit bot identity, hop limits and coordination only when unattended Weaver assignment is actually proposed; the present on-demand and confirmation decisions stay in force.
- **REQ-023.** The draft PR is narrowed to an explicit next action or wait with the source link, using TASK-129 and existing PR links as the baseline. A full timeline is optional follow-up. Preserve unknown, stale and conflicting states and do not claim an open PR is itself a request.
- **Agent-base.** Adoption is a subsequent goal after the Weaver/PALab work and validation. This proposal does not change that repository.
